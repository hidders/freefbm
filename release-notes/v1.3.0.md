# FreeFBM 1.3

FreeFBM 1.3 draws ORM2 diagrams the way NORMA does and exchanges models with NORMA in both directions: `.orm` files can now be exported as well as imported, derivation rules and join paths included. It also adds population sentences, German verbalisation and a choice of model language. This release covers everything since 1.2 (9 November 2026).

## ⚠️ Files

- Files from 1.2 open in 1.3. Files saved by 1.3 are not meant to be opened in 1.2. Role connectors are no longer stored in the file (they follow from where the shapes are, see below), so 1.2 would draw some of them to the wrong shape of the object type.
- When an older file opens, its stored role connectors are dropped. Where an object type is drawn more than once on a diagram, a role may now connect to a nearer shape than before.

## Diagrams that look like NORMA's

- Shapes are drawn at NORMA's sizes, pens and colours: dark blue object types, black role boxes and text, purple constraints, blue role names. Diagram text is in a Tahoma-compatible font, so names and readings are as wide as in NORMA.
- External constraint symbols have NORMA's proportions. External frequency constraints stay pills, with NORMA's pen and text.
- Role names, readings, value ranges and frequency markers are placed where NORMA puts them. Forward and reverse readings are shown together as in NORMA.
- Uniqueness bars are stacked as in NORMA, with a little more room between the role boxes and the first bar, so a deontic bar's circle no longer touches the boxes.
- The uniqueness constraint that identifies an objectified fact type gets a solid stub from its bar to the frame. A double bar again only means that the bar identifies the player of the role it leaves out.
- **Display as object type** (Inspector › Display) draws an objectified fact type as an object type box on one diagram.
- **Subtype links** can be shown per shape: all, from subtypes, to supertypes or none (Inspector › Subtype links).
- The diagram background is parchment by default, or white as in NORMA (Inspector settings › Diagram background).
- 100% zoom is now what 80% was before.

## Role connectors follow the shapes

- A role connects to the nearest shape of its player, as in NORMA. Moving, adding or removing a shape can move connectors, and a connector that moves is highlighted briefly.
- Removing an object type from a diagram no longer removes the fact types it plays in. Its roles connect to another shape or are left unconnected.
- Copy and paste take exactly what is selected. The Browser's + adds a fact type on its own, and Add with players adds its players too.
- **Reveal player** (double-click a role box, or its right-click menu) adds the player of an unconnected role next to it.

## NORMA export and import

- **File › Export NORMA .orm…** writes the active ORM2 schema as a NORMA file: object types, data types and reference modes, fact types and readings, all constraints with their shapes, ranges, notes, groups with colours, custom properties and diagram tabs. A file imported from NORMA comes back with NORMA's own ids and shape positions.
- **Derivation rules and join paths** are written as NORMA role paths, including negation, disjunction (Or and Xor), value conditions, recursion, comparisons, calculations and aggregates, with the functions they use defined in the model. Rules NORMA can't express are kept as informal rules holding FreeFBM's formula. Exported files have been checked to load in NORMA.
- What FreeFBM could not export is listed in the export report and in one summary note on the first diagram. Remarks about single elements go into their NORMA notes.
- **File › Import NORMA .orm…** now sits beside the export (File › Open still accepts `.orm` files).
- The import reads aggregates and subqueries in role paths, keeps NORMA's tab order, places fact types at the middle of their role boxes, and keeps groups exactly as NORMA wrote them.

## Building constraints

- While you pick a constraint's roles, the Inspector shows what to click next, why a click was refused, and Done and Cancel. Candidate role boxes are tinted.
- Internal uniqueness and frequency constraints start from a role box. Their modality, preferred identifier and frequency range are set in the Inspector.
- Arrow keys move a selected range or frequency marker.
- Fact type right-click menus show display options (roles reversed, shown vertically, collapsed) as tickable items, with remove and delete last.

## Languages and verbalisation

- **Population sentences**: the Population tab's Table | Sentences switch writes each fact and instance as a sentence, such as "Student 'P1' enrols in Course 'DB1'", in all languages. Objectified fact types can have a nominal reading ("the enrolment of Student 'P1' in Course 'DB1'").
- **German** verbalisation, with cases and genders, joins English, Dutch, French, Italian, Spanish and Polish.
- **Choose the model language**: the language switch can make the language you are viewing the model's own, saved in the file.

## Smaller changes

- A file opens on its first diagram.
- PDF export: labels that reach past their shapes are no longer cut off, notes and link fact types are included, and shapes keep their colours.
- The diagram tab bar's buttons stay in place while the tabs scroll.
- Fixed reference mode labels of compound names, and overlapping ring symbols in the Inspector.
